Investment Overview

Executive summary and investment thesis for Anchor & Vine Hospitality Group

Anchor & Vine Hospitality Group represents a rare opportunity to acquire a highly profitable, strategically positioned collection of three award-winning waterfront boutique hotels on Florida's Gulf Coast. With a 14-year track record of exceptional operational execution, strong unit economics, and demonstrated pricing power, the company is positioned as the #2 independent operator by RevPAR in its region.

Key Investment Highlights

  • Proven Operating Model: 14-year history of disciplined growth from 1 to 3 properties with consistent profitability and operational excellence
  • Strong Unit Economics: 92% occupancy, $186 RevPAR, 27.8% adjusted EBITDA margin, and 41% repeat guest rate demonstrate pricing power and operational efficiency
  • Diversified Revenue: 62.7% rooms, 34.0% food & beverage (award-winning on-site dining), 3.3% spa and ancillary services provide revenue stability
  • Premier Assets: Three freehold waterfront properties in high-value Gulf Coast markets (Clearwater, Sarasota, Naples); all renovated within the past 5 years; two properties hold AAA Four Diamond certification
  • Exceptional Guest Loyalty: 4.7/5.0 rating across 6,200+ guest reviews; 41% repeat guest rate significantly exceeds industry benchmarks
  • Scalable Operations: Standardized SOPs and training programs enable new property integration within 90 days; unified brand identity since 2019 rebrand
  • Attractive Valuation: $8.5M asking price represents 2.15x FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA with 90-day seller transition consulting period

Financial Summary

Anchor & Vine delivered strong and consistent revenue growth, increasing from $11.8M in FY2023 to $14.2M in FY2025, a 20.3% CAGR. Adjusted EBITDA of $3.95M represents a margin expansion of 240 basis points year-over-year, driven by disciplined cost management and revenue optimization across the portfolio.

The company maintains a conservative capital structure with $2.1M outstanding mortgage debt at 5.4% fixed across 2 properties, maturing in 2031. This debt represents only 26.7% of the asking price, creating significant equity value for the acquirer.

The Enterprise

Company overview, properties, competitive positioning, and strategic strengths

Company Overview & Heritage

Anchor & Vine Hospitality Group was founded in 2011 by hospitality operators Maria Chen and David Okoye, who transformed a single boutique waterfront property into a regionally respected three-property collection. Over 14 years, the company has established itself as the #2 independent operator in the Gulf Coast region by RevPAR, achieving this position through unwavering commitment to guest experience, operational excellence, and authentic local partnerships.

100% privately held with no outside investors, Anchor & Vine remains founder-led and focused on long-term value creation rather than short-term financial optimization. The company's core values—guest-first service, sustainable sourcing, and community partnership—are embedded throughout its operations.

  • Event & Wedding Venues: Each property hosts weddings, corporate retreats, and private events; strong reputation for elegant waterfront settings
  • Spa Services: Wellness and spa offerings at 2 of 3 properties (Clearwater, Sarasota); expansion to Naples under consideration

Competitive Advantages

Anchor & Vine's competitive position rests on several durable, difficult-to-replicate advantages:

1. Prime Waterfront Locations: Each property commands freehold waterfront real estate in high-demand Gulf Coast markets, providing strategic pricing power and insulation from new branded competition.

2. Operational Consistency: Long-tenured general managers at each property (averaging 8+ years tenure) embody the company's culture and guest standards.

3. Brand Reputation: Unified "Anchor & Vine" brand identity since 2019 has created strong recognition among leisure and event guests; 4.7/5.0 rating across 6,200+ reviews.

4. Guest Loyalty: 41% repeat guest rate significantly exceeds industry benchmarks, reducing reliance on costly acquisition channels and providing revenue visibility.

5. Scalable Operating Model: Standardized SOPs and training programs enable efficient integration of new properties within 90 days.

Risk Considerations

Key risks to business performance and mitigation considerations

Competitive Risks

  • New Branded Competitor: A branded hotel operator is entering the Sarasota market in 2027 with significant capital backing and national brand recognition. Risk: potential share loss among corporate/group segments. Mitigation: Anchor & Vine's waterfront location, established reputation, and event capabilities provide defensibility; focus on leisure and wedding segments less affected by branded competition.
  • OTA Dependency: 35% of bookings via OTAs; continued commission inflation or algorithm changes could impact margins. Mitigation: Direct website bookings (38%) and loyalty/repeat business (27%) provide diversification; ongoing SEO and content investment to drive direct bookings.

Financial Risks

  • F&B Cost Inflation: Food costs exposed to regional inflation and commodity price volatility. Current F&B COGS at 28% of F&B revenue; 500 bps inflation would pressure margins. Mitigation: Annual vendor contracts; menu engineering and pricing adjustments; cost controls have historically offset inflation.
  • Debt Service Obligations: $2.1M mortgage at 5.4% fixed through 2031; obligation to service regardless of business performance. Mitigation: Conservative leverage (0.53x Debt/EBITDA); strong cash flow coverage of debt service (~8x interest coverage).

Operational Risks

  • Hurricane / Weather Exposure: Gulf Coast waterfront properties subject to seasonal hurricane risk, flooding, and severe weather. Potential for temporary closures and property damage. Mitigation: Comprehensive $15M insurance coverage; properties have withstood historical hurricanes; coastal location provides resort revenue appeal that offsets weather risk.
  • Labor Market Tightness: Hospitality industry experiencing wage inflation and staffing challenges. Cost pressure on 31% labor ratio. Mitigation: Strong culture and retention (24% turnover vs. 35% industry) reduce recruitment and training costs; benefits packages competitive with regional operators.
  • Key Person Dependency: Founder-led operations; CEO and COO integral to strategy, culture, and stakeholder relationships. Succession plan in early stages. Mitigation: VP Operations identified as COO successor; 90-day transition consulting from founders post-sale; management team bench strength through Priya Anand (CFO, 6 years) and long-tenured property GMs.

Market & Demand Risks

  • Leisure Segment Concentration: 68% of bookings from leisure travelers; economic downturn could reduce travel demand. Mitigation: 41% repeat guest rate and strong brand loyalty provide demand stability; diversification into corporate and events reduce leisure dependency.
  • Seasonality: Gulf Coast properties experience seasonal demand fluctuations (peak winter, slower summer). Revenue concentration in Q4 and Q1. Mitigation: Events and wedding business extends season; F&B and spa services provide counter-seasonal revenue; occupancy management strategies optimize shoulder seasons.

Legal & Regulatory Risks

  • Regulatory Changes: Potential changes to liquor regulations, environmental rules, or hospitality licensing could increase compliance costs. Mitigation: Current full compliance with all regulations; established relationships with local authorities and legal counsel.
  • Litigation: No current litigation; hospitality operations inherently subject to potential guest injury or employment claims. Mitigation: Comprehensive $15M insurance coverage; professional HR and risk management practices.

Integration & Acquisition Risks

  • Change Management: New ownership may cause management/staff transition concerns; potential employee turnover during transition. Mitigation: 90-day founder consulting period to ensure continuity; retention agreements for key staff; clear communication of strategy and vision.
  • Systems Integration: If acquirer operates different tech stack, integration of property management and revenue systems could be disruptive. Mitigation: Cloudbeds and revenue management systems widely used; integration should be straightforward.
